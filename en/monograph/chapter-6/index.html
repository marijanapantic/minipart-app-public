Chapter 6

Concluding Remarks: Participation, Legitimacy, and the Governance of Extractive Transitions

  • Tamara Maričić1
  • Marijana Pantić1
  • Ana Perić2
  1. 1Institute of Architecture and Urban & Spatial Planning of Serbia, Serbia
  2. 2University College Dublin, School of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Policy, Ireland

Abstract

This concluding chapter synthesises the key findings of the volume through the lenses of participatory governance, justice, legitimacy, and multi-level governance. Drawing on international case studies from extractive and transitional regions, it highlights the persistent gap between formal participation procedures and meaningful citizen influence over decision-making. The analysis identifies power and knowledge asymmetries, limited institutional responsiveness, and insufficient coordination across governance levels as major barriers to effective participation. It advocates a relational and justice-oriented approach that integrates procedural, distributive, and recognition justice with continuous dialogue, transparent feedback, and collaborative decision-making. The chapter concludes that meaningful participation is essential for building trust, strengthening institutional legitimacy, preventing conflicts, and supporting socially just and sustainable transitions in mining regions.

Keywords:

participatory planning; mining regions; stakeholder engagement; practice.
