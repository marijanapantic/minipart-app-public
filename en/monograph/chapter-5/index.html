Chapter 5

Bridging Citizens’ and Institutions’ Perspectives in Serbia: Participation in the Spatial Planning of Mining Regions

  • Marijana Pantić1
  • Dušanka Milosavljević2
  • Milena Toković3
  • Tamara Maričić1
  • Milovan Vuković4
  1. 1Institute of Architecture and Urban & Spatial Planning of Serbia, Serbia
  2. 2University of Belgrade – Institute for Philosophy and Social Theory, Serbia
  3. 3University of Belgrade – Faculty of Geography, Serbia
  4. 4University of Belgrade – Technical Faculty in Bor, Serbia

Abstract

Despite extensive scholarship on participatory planning, limited attention has been paid to the persistent gap between formal participatory frameworks and citizens' engagement and perceptions in mining regions. Public participation in spatial and urban planning of mining regions has been examined through conceptual lenses that emphasise power relations, governance processes, motivation drivers, and effectiveness of participatory methods. The case study draws on three theoretical pillars - participatory governance and perception gaps, motivation theories in public participation, and participatory planning methods - to interpret findings from a quantitative survey and qualitative interviews with institutional actors. These pillars support analysis of turnout, motivations for (non)participation, and preferred methods, as well as misalignments between citizens' and institutional perceptions, with emphasis on mining areas. The study employs a mixed-methods approach, relying primarily on primary data. The quantitative component uses survey data from residents in the Bor and Majdanpek mining region, capturing awareness, motivation, and engagement with formal planning procedures. The qualitative component consisted of in-depth interviews with planners, national and local authorities, mining companies, and NGOs, providing insights into institutional perspectives on participation, barriers, and practices. This enables a nuanced understanding of participation dynamics in resource extraction areas in Serbia, highlighting systemic constraints and opportunities for engagement. The study contributes to debates on participatory planning by linking empirical evidence to conceptual frameworks, emphasising citizen awareness, comprehension, and feedback mechanisms in meaningful involvement of relevant actors in resource-rich regions.

Keywords:

public participation; spatial planning; participatory governance; mining regions; Serbia.
