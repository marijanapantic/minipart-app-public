Chapter 3

Cultural Planning as a Participatory Method for Resource Peripheries: The Case of Peat Extraction in Ilomantsi, Finland

  • Simo Häyrynen1
  • Juha Kotilainen1
  • Larissa Huohvanainen1
  • Jani Karhu1
  1. 1University of Eastern Finland, Department of Geographical and Historical Studies, Finland

Abstract

This chapter investigates public participation in spatial planning in Ilomantsi, a peripheral mining region in Finland facing both underground gold mining and peat extraction. While peat extraction has a significant environmental impact and is targeted for phase-out under EU sustainability and Just Transition policies, local opinions are divided: some see resource extraction as an economic lifeline, others prioritise ecological restoration. The research draws on the Bog 2.0 project, which implements comprehensive sustainability by integrating ecological, socio-economic, political, and cultural dimensions. Empirical data were collected through action research and interviews in order to explore perceptions of environmental impact and participatory processes. Findings show that, despite Finland's strong democratic traditions, formal participation is often dominated by a few active voices, leaving many residents uncertain and underrepresented. To address this, the study examines cultural empowerment, an action research approach combining historical mapping, interviews, and participatory cultural activities - such as theatre, a short film, and cultural branding - to broaden dialogue and co-create shared understanding. Early applications suggest that this method enhances residents' sense of belonging, legitimises them as stakeholders, and fosters more inclusive, context-sensitive planning. The chapter highlights both the challenges and potential of integrating innovative participatory approaches into sustainability transitions in peripheral resource-dependent communities.

Keywords:

cultural empowerment; public participation; action research; resource extraction; Finland; Ilomantsi.
